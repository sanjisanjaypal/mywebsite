Aster Bank: eLearning that shows wealth teams a better working day

An international bank in Singapore was rolling out a new way of working for its wealth management teams. I designed the UI of the eLearning modules that introduced it: a course player and a set of illustrated screens that follow one relationship manager through his day, before and after the change.

Aster Bank (white-labelled)
Industry

Impact

A change programme explained as one person’s working day

  • 15short screens per module, each carrying a single idea
  • 1relationship manager, Kevin, whose day carries the whole story
  • 3help tools on every screen: Resource, Glossary and Help

In short

Problem

A new way of working for wealth teams is hard to explain in slides and policy documents. People needed to see why it mattered to their own day.

Approach

Tell it as a story. Follow one relationship manager through a familiar day, show the friction, then show the same day with the new workbench.

Result

A consistent course player and a flat, illustrated screen system the learning team could reuse across modules.

Context

A transformation that people had to understand, not just attend

The bank’s wealth management business was moving its relationship managers onto a new workbench: one place for portfolio performance, reports, appointments, calls and events, with research and portfolio reporting built in.

Training had to reach busy front-line staff, so it needed to be short, self-paced and clear about the benefit before it asked anyone to learn a new tool.

  • An abstract change programme with many moving parts
  • Busy staff with little time for training
  • Banking terms that newer staff might not know
  • Several modules that had to look and work alike
  • Desktop and touch screens

Business goal

Help wealth teams understand and adopt the new way of working.

Learner goal

Quickly see what changes in my day and why it is better.

Design goal

One player and one visual language that every module can reuse.

Audience

Relationship managers and the teams around them

The learners were the people who would use the workbench every day, so the story had to look and sound like their own desk, their own clients and their own to-do list.

  • Relationship managers
  • Wealth team leads
  • Support and service staff
  • New joiners

Learning approach

Show the day, then show the difference

  • A persona to followKevin, a relationship manager, gives learners someone to recognise. Every screen is a moment in his day.
  • Familiar momentsMarket research, today’s tasks, a client who wants to invest in real estate, a pile of client files: the everyday work the change affects.
  • Before and afterThe same desk shown twice. Before: manual effort, collating reports, coordinating over calls and emails. After: information anytime and anywhere, automated tasks, more time for clients.
  • The tool, in contextThe workbench appears as Kevin’s own checklist, ticking off portfolio performance and appointments against today’s tasks.
  • Short screensOne idea per screen and a visible page count, so learners always know how far they have to go.

Storyboard

Eight beats, sketched before any screen was drawn

I storyboarded the module as a sequence of beats, so the learning team could agree on the story and the order before visual design began.

Course player

A frame that stays still so the story can move

Every module runs inside the same player. Controls never move, so learners spend their attention on the scene, not on finding the next button.

  • Learners lose their placePage count and a segmented progress bar on every screen
  • Banking terms slow new staff downGlossary always in the top bar
  • Some learners use touch screensA swipe hint and large, simple targets
  • Learners want to keep notesA notes button in the same corner on every screen

Visual language

Flat illustration, one desk, a few strong colours

A top-down view of Kevin’s desk is the recurring stage: the same monitor, keyboard, notebook and plant, so learners always know where they are. Vignette cards titled “Today at Kevin’s desk” zoom into single moments, each in one dominant colour.

Design principles

What guided every screen

  • Story before software. Learners meet the problem in Kevin’s day before they meet the tool.
  • One idea per screen. A headline, one illustration and a one-line caption.
  • Familiar over flashy. Real tasks and real requests from a relationship manager’s week.
  • Colour with a job. Dark for the old way, bright for the new; each vignette owns one colour.
  • A frame that never moves. The same player and controls in every module.

Reflection

What I learned, and what I’d add next

  • Training is a product too. The same UX basics apply: know the learner, cut the noise, show progress.
  • Stories travel. A single persona made an abstract programme easy to explain across teams.
  • Next, I’d add practice. A short scenario where learners complete Kevin’s tasks in the workbench themselves.
  • And measure it. Completion rates and a quick confidence check before and after each module.

The bank has been white-labelled as Aster Bank, and its colours and logo have been replaced. Screens are shown for portfolio purposes.
